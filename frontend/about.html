What the game is

Players are on one of seven teams. The map is divided into squares roughly 300 meters across. You claim a square for your team by being in it and hearing the mesh from it. Teams take squares from each other. A season runs six months and the team holding the most squares at the end wins — but every calendar month inside it has its own winner and its own honors, so there is always something close to play for. See the results.

The seven teams
RED GREEN BLUE PURPLE YELLOW ORANGE PINK

Every player picks one of these seven colors, and every team competes for the same ground.

How scoring works
  1. You claim a square by hearing repeaters from it. Each repeater a ping hears is worth a tenth of a point, up to one point per ping.
  2. A ping that hears no repeaters scores nothing and claims nothing. Being somewhere is not enough — you have to reach the mesh.
  3. The first team to paint an unclaimed square takes it.
  4. A square that has just been captured cannot be taken for fifteen minutes, no matter what.
  5. After that it falls to whoever out-scores the team currently holding it. Only the holder's score matters, not every other team's.
  6. The first time a new person paints a square for their team, it is worth an extra half point. More people beats one person going back and forth.
  7. The same person cannot repaint the same square within five minutes.
  8. Scores fade over time, so held ground has to be revisited.
Net check-ins

There's a weekly net on Wednesday evenings, 17:00 to 23:59 Boise time. Checking in during that window earns your team 25 points, on top of any ground you hold — a check-in before 17:00 or after midnight doesn't count.

You can check in once per net. Posting five times doesn't earn five times — it earns once, same as everyone else.

Turn up week after week and each check-in is worth more. The bonus starts on your second net in a row and grows until it caps:

  • 1st net in a row — 25 points
  • 2nd — 30
  • 3rd — 35
  • 4th — 40
  • 5th — 45
  • 6th and every one after — 50

Miss a net and you start again at 25. A week where the net doesn't happen at all costs you nothing — your run picks up where it left off.

Points count toward your team's total alongside squares, so a night on the net is a real contribution whether or not you ever leave the house. Wardriving and checking in are two ways to play, not two kinds of player. People who take ground are Wardrivers and people who work the net are Phreaks, but that's flavour, not a class you pick — do both and you'll show up on both leaderboards.

To be credited, your radio has to be registered to you. On Meshtastic that's your node ID. On MeshCore it's your contact ID, which MeshMapper binds for you automatically if you wardrive, or you can pick your node from the list when you join.

Every month has a winner

A six-month season is a long time to wait to find out how you did, so each calendar month closes with its own result. A month is judged on that month alone — ground taken and points earned between its own dates — not on the season standings, so falling behind early never puts the next month out of reach.

Alongside the team standings, each month hands out honors:

  • Month Winner — the team that gained the most points that month.
  • Top Attacker — took the most squares off other teams. There's a version of this for each team too.
  • Top Defender — won back the most squares your team had lost. Also awarded per team.
  • Explorer — claimed the most squares nobody had ever claimed. Easy early in a season, harder as the map fills in.
  • Frontier — claimed the most squares more than twenty miles beyond any town. Some months nobody manages one at all.
  • Top Phreak — earned the most check-in points.
  • Most Consistent — held the longest run of nets.
  • Quick Fingers — checked in fastest after the net opened, on average.

Two things don't count toward Explorer or Frontier: squares claimed while moving at aircraft speed, and squares inside a town for Frontier. Those two awards are about reach and effort, and flying over the back country is neither.

The two protocols
MeshCore Live

Playable now. Your position comes from the MeshMapper wardriving app, which forwards a copy of your wardrive to MeshWars.

Meshtastic Live

Playable now, on the same seven-team, registered-player, grid-square game as MeshCore. Register your node ID on the join page, then squares are claimed wherever your node's broadcast position is heard by an MQTT feeder.

How to join

Registration and MeshMapper setup happen on the join page. Join MeshWars to get started.

Where it's played

MeshWars is currently scoped for the western US and Texas. If you'd like to join the fun or need help, please reach out to malice in the Intermountain Mesh Discord.
