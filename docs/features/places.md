# Places Worth Going

What follows is the design conversation written down so it does not have to be had again, plus what the build actually does where that differs from the plan.

## The idea

Every square is currently worth the same; the map is graph paper with no features. Named places — summits, parks, landmarks — are destinations worth more than the cell they sit in. None of the lists need inventing: Summits on the Air and Parks on the Air are curated ham-radio programmes, and landmarks come from OpenStreetMap.

## Values and the cap

**Scored by effort, not by category (changed 2026-08-25).** The original model paid a flat value by reference type — every park was worth 25, whether it sat across a parking lot or an hour up a dirt road. That said the walk and the trip were the same errand. The value now depends on whether the place is inside a town's own limits:

| | Landmark | Park | Summit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inside city limits | 5 | 5 | 5 |
| Outside city limits | 10 | 25 | 50-100, scaled by elevation |

"City limits" is computed at seed-build time, not guessed at runtime: `app/reference/places.csv` carries the Census anchors this feature already uses for "how far is the nearest town" — each row is `lat,lon,effective_radius_m`, where the radius is `sqrt(ALAND/pi)`, a circle of the same land area as the place, standing in for its limits. A place is **inside** city limits if it falls within that radius of *any* anchor. `scripts/build_places_seed.py`'s `score_points()` (run once, in the `merge` stage) checks every place against every anchor and bakes the resulting value straight into the seed CSV's `points` column — the loader and the scoring path need no new logic, they already just read `points` off the row.

The anchors come from two US Census 2024 Gazetteer files, built by `scripts/build_places_csv.py` (fetch-place, fetch-ua, build): the PLACE file (incorporated cities/towns and Census Designated Places, no type filter — an unincorporated CDP is as valid an anchor as an incorporated city) and, since 2026-09-07, the URBAN AREA file. Place rows alone left real gaps: San Francisco's corporate limits legally include the Farallon Islands, a non-contiguous exclave ~48km offshore, so Census's one interior point for that whole shape lands out at sea rather than on the mainland; the Las Vegas Strip sits in unincorporated Clark County (Winchester/Paradise CDPs), outside the City of Las Vegas's own boundary and short of neighboring anchors' circles too; New Orleans city is coterminous with a parish whose limits reach across most of Lake Pontchartrain, dragging its interior point away from the French Quarter; Orlando's decades of annexation pull its interior point toward large southeastern parcels instead of downtown. All four read as "remote" despite sitting in the middle of a metro area. Urban areas are drawn from contiguous population density rather than jurisdiction lines, so one urban-area anchor per metro closes gaps like these generally instead of hand-patching individual cities. See the script's module docstring for the full mechanism and `tests/test_places_anchors.py` for the regression coverage.

**Scope is the 50 states + DC, not a play-area bbox (changed 2026-09-07).** The anchor set used to be filtered to the MeshWars play area (roughly the western US) plus a 1-degree margin, matching a play-area check that existed elsewhere in the app. That check is disabled in production and the board is world-open, so `scripts/build_places_csv.py` now keeps every Census place/urban-area row in the 50 states + DC and applies no geographic filter beyond that — Puerto Rico, Guam, the US Virgin Islands, American Samoa, and the Northern Mariana Islands are excluded (all appear in the source Census files but "50 states plus DC" was the explicit scope), and non-US anchors are out of scope entirely (the Census Gazetteer is a US-only source; going international would need a different data source, not a wider filter on this one). The anchor count grew from 13,024 (western play area only) to 34,652 (national).

**A park with a matched boundary is scored from its own shape, not one point on it (changed 2026-09-07, "Yosemite pays the same as a pocket park").** The rule above — is the place's own point inside a town's circle — is fine for a point-sized landmark, but for a park with a real boundary it let one arbitrary interior point decide the rate for the whole polygon. That paid Yosemite National Park, Flathead National Forest, Humboldt-Toiyabe National Forest, Payette National Forest, and Sequoia National Forest (all several thousand km² and genuinely remote) the flat 5-point in-city rate, because their POTA centre point happened to land inside some small nearby town's circle purely by chance. This is deliberately **not** a size threshold — a park is not worth more for being big, only for being hard to reach, which is why a large park that sits entirely inside a city (Golden Gate Park, Central Park) must still pay the in-city rate rather than the remote one. Instead, `scripts/build_places_seed.py`'s `match_parks()`/`fetch_padus_parks()` measure what fraction of the park's own **true, full boundary** (before any storage clip — see below) lies outside every nearby town's circle: more than half outside scores the remote rate (`remote_by_area`), otherwise the in-city rate (`in_city_by_area`). A park `match_parks()` never found a boundary for keeps the plain point test above (there is nothing else to test); so does every landmark, which never has a boundary at all. The 50% cutoff (`PARK_REMOTE_AREA_FRAC`) is the plain reading of "substantially outside" and was not tuned to force any particular case's answer — see that constant's own comment in `scripts/build_places_seed.py` for the measurements behind it, including Rocky Mountain Arsenal National Wildlife Refuge, a genuinely borderline case reported with its measured fraction rather than adjusted to land anywhere in particular.

The boundary this test runs against must be the park's real, pre-clip shape: `match_parks()` stores a version of the matched boundary clipped to a ~6km buffer around the park's own point (so a Wetland Management District or a Refuge that bundles scattered parcels across a whole region doesn't ship an oversized geometry) — a clipped geometry would just reproduce the old centroid bug with extra steps, so this test runs against the full, unclipped polygon before that clip is ever applied.

**A matched boundary itself can be wrong, separately from its size (added 2026-09-07, "a roadside museum does not have a 21,000 km² boundary").** `match_parks()`'s matching rule accepts a candidate polygon on any shared name token as long as it contains the POTA point — deliberately lenient, so a park gets an approximate boundary rather than none. That occasionally attaches a huge, genuinely-named PAD-US polygon to an unrelated park whose centre point happens to fall inside it and which shares one common word with its name and nothing more — confirmed in the shipped seed for "Oklahoma Route 66 Museum State Historic Site" and "Cherokee Hills Scenic Byway Scenic Site", both matched to 18,000-21,000 km² boundaries this way. A sanity check rejects a match only when **both** the matched area is implausible for a real boundary (over `MATCH_AREA_SANITY_CEILING_M2`, 15,000 km²) **and** the name evidence behind it was weak (`MATCH_AREA_SANITY_MIN_SCORE`, under 0.5) — area alone is never enough, since a genuinely enormous, well-named match (Flathead National Forest's own boundary, or a Wetland Management District's real multi-parcel bundle) scores 1.0 on the same name test and is never touched regardless of size. A rejected match falls back to a point place (same as one `match_parks()` never found at all) rather than being dropped from the seed.

**The dominant bad-boundary failure is inheriting a containing unit's polygon, not a stray name-token match (continued 2026-09-07).** The check above only ever compares one candidate polygon to its own name score, so it cannot see the more common failure: a small, specifically-named feature (a fish hatchery, a natural bridge) whose winning match is actually the boundary of the much larger unit it physically sits inside — byte-identical to whatever legitimate row separately matched that same polygon under its own name. Confirmed in the shipped seed: "Tonto State Fish Hatchery" and "Tonto Natural Bridge State Park" both carried Tonto National Forest's own 11,601.6 km² boundary. Three more passes run over the whole set of matched parks after the per-candidate check above, all in `scripts/build_places_seed.py`'s `_clean_matched_park_boundaries()`, scoped to matches ≥100 km² (below that, a bad or duplicate match is not the multi-county-scale credit-zone risk this cleanup targets):

- **Duplicate geometry** (`_resolve_duplicate_boundary_group()`): when two or more matched parks carry the exact same PAD-US area, at most one of them can really own it. Each name is ranked into one of three tiers — an unmistakably large-format designation (National Forest/Park/Monument/Grassland, Wilderness, National Conservation or Recreation Area) outranks an ordinary one (a Wildlife Management Area, a National Wildlife Refuge, a BLM Recreation/Herd Management Area — these vary too much in real size to trust either way), which in turn outranks a point-scale one. Whichever single member sits alone at the group's highest tier keeps the boundary; a tie at any tier (including two point-scale names tied at the bottom, or two ordinary names with nothing to separate them) means the whole group **keeps** the boundary instead of losing it (changed 2026-09-07, reverting the fail-safe's original direction) — a tie cannot be broken without ground truth this seed does not carry (point-in-polygon cannot help either: a matched park's point IS its boundary's centroid, so it is trivially inside its own polygon regardless of which member truly owns the ground), and the two failure costs are not symmetric. Keeping every tied member leaves co-located real places sharing one credit zone — minor, and reversible later with a PAD-US-backed pass. Stripping every tied member deletes real, named wilderness (or a national forest, or both units of a national monument) from the board outright — confirmed in the shipped seed for Teton Wilderness Area / Jedediah Smith Wilderness Area, Sam Houston National Forest, both Bears Ears National Monument units, and others, all wrongly stripped by the original fail-safe direction and restored by this change.
- **Designation vs. scale**: a museum, fish hatchery, natural bridge, scenic byway/site, historic site, visitor/interpretive center, picnic area, trailhead, or campground should never own a boundary over 500 km² (`POINT_SCALE_AREA_CEILING_M2`) — comfortably above Fort Sill National Historic Site's own legitimate 379.3 km² (an entire Army post) and below every confirmed-bogus case measured. This is a keyword-gated ceiling, not a blanket one, and also catches a point-scale name with no duplicate to compare against.
- **Administrative envelope**: a Wetland Management District's boundary is genuine, not inherited — but it is a multi-county, sometimes multi-state administrative footprint of scattered easement parcels, not contiguous ground anyone walks onto. Stripped unconditionally regardless of size. (Individual "National Waterfowl Production Area" units are left alone — every one in this seed is already parcel-sized.)

A small number of matches pass all three checks above yet are still wrong — "San Bernard National Wildlife Refuge" matched an 8,323.8 km² polygon (the real refuge is ~110 km²), has no duplicate partner anywhere in this seed to rank against, and "National Wildlife Refuge" is not a point-scale designation (Desert, Cabeza Prieta, Charles M. Russell, and Sheldon NWRs are all legitimately thousands of km² and correctly kept). These are tracked by ref_code in `_KNOWN_BOGUS_BOUNDARY_MATCHES`, confirmed by direct spot check rather than by any rule the script can run on its own.

A full PAD-US rebuild was not available when this cleanup landed, so `app/reference/places_worth_going.csv` was patched in place with the same logic: 39 park rows lose their boundary under the corrected duplicate-geometry direction (22 duplicate geometry, 12 administrative envelope, 4 designation-vs-scale, 1 known bad match), dropping matched-park boundary area from 1,378,362 km² to 1,068,000 km² across the seed. None were deleted — every one still stands as a point place.

**A remote summit scales with elevation (changed 2026-08-25, "lets make the points for peaks scaling. 50 for low elevation peaks up to 100 for 9000ft +").** Every summit used to pay the same flat 100 whether it was a modest hill or Idaho's highest peak — a SOTA 8-point-or-better summit could be genuinely small (see the threshold note below) and still be worth exactly as much as Borah Peak. Now a remote summit scales linearly from **50 points at 6,000ft up to 100 points at 9,000ft and above**, clamped at both ends and rounded to a whole point. **The in-city rule still wins outright** — `score_points()` checks in-city containment first, for every `ref_type` including summit, so a summit inside a town's limits is worth 5 regardless of how tall it is (you can park at it); only a summit that fails that check goes on to the elevation scale.

The 6,000ft floor is measured, not assumed: joining SOTA's own `AltFt` onto the 7,987 active remote summits in the seed at the time this landed gave a min of 2,110ft, p10 6,761ft, median 8,992ft, p90 12,540ft, max 14,494ft — a real gap in the low end, not a smooth taper. Only 16 summits fell in the 4,000-5,999ft band; the main body of the distribution starts at 6,000ft (1,099 summits alone in 6,000-6,999ft), with p1 landing at 6,002ft — right at that seam. A tiny cluster of 80 summits (1.0% of remote summits) sits below 6,000ft — these clear `SUMMIT_MIN_SOTA_POINTS`'s prominence bar on relief above flat surrounding terrain despite low absolute elevation — and they clamp to the floor of 50 rather than scaling below it. The 9,000ft ceiling is Matt's own figure, not derived, and happens to land almost exactly on the measured median, so close to half of today's remote summits reach the full 100.

Elevation itself is a new column, `elevation_ft`, carried on the `place` table (summit only, `NULL` for park/landmark) purely so an operator can see *why* a summit scored what it did without re-deriving it — `scripts/build_places_seed.py`'s `fetch_sota()` reads it straight from SOTA's own `AltFt` column; a SOTA row missing it is dropped from the seed outright rather than kept with a blank.

A second CSV column, `points_reason` (`in_city` / `remote` / `remote_scaled`), rides along purely so the reason is visible later — the `place` table carries it too, and the admin preview's `/api/admin/places/preview` sample rows include it — but nothing at runtime branches on it; `points` is still the only number that decides a credit. `remote_scaled` marks a summit whose value came from the elevation curve above, distinct from `remote`'s flat park/landmark value.

A remote park is still worth exactly one check-in — that is the anchor for anything genuinely away from town. A single summit at or above 9,000ft still reaches the whole weekly ceiling of 100 points on its own; a lower one takes a second place alongside it. A town's own landmarks, parks, and even its in-bounds summits are worth a nickel apiece, so the choice for someone actually travelling is still how they would rather spend the week rather than which nets more.

## Rules

- One credit per reference, per person, per week.
- 100 points per person per week, whatever the mix. The cap clamps rather than refuses: a place credits for whatever is left of the week, even when that is less than its full value. At 50 points for the week, a 100-point summit still credits — for the remaining 50, taking you to the cap. Only when nothing is left at all (already exactly at 100) does a place credit nothing and leave no record of the attempt. A place that paid out a partial amount this way has used its one credit for the week; it does not sit there for the difference to be claimed later the same week.
- Places do NOT stack. One square can map to several places — a landmark standing inside a big park is the ordinary case — and only the HIGHEST-VALUE live one credits. The lesser place is dropped outright: it does not pay alongside the winner, whether the winner pays in full or is clamped by the weekly cap, and it does not step in if the winner was already credited this week (in which case that square simply pays nothing this week). Equal point values are broken by a stable hash of the place id (`app/place_scoring._stable_tiebreak`), so the same square always resolves to the same winner and insertion order never decides it. Stacking was the behaviour until 2026-08-27 and was never intended — one ping paid twice for one errand.
- The week resets Wednesday just before the net, so the game has one clock — the gathering and the reset are the same moment.
- Activating requires a scoring ping; being there is not enough, exactly as it is not enough anywhere else.
- Points go to a personal Explorer Score AND to the team total, the same shape check-ins already have. The Explorer Score is season-long and feeds a player's total score, so it is never month-scoped or reset — Explorer is an activity and a rankings tab, not an honor, the same shape NetOps has.
- Aircraft are excluded.

## The seed, as actually pulled

Sources ended up simpler than "a narrow OpenStreetMap tag list" alone implied: summits are SOTA's own list, parks are POTA's, and landmarks are OpenStreetMap filtered to the tag list below — all three pulled 2026-08-24 (`scripts/build_places_seed.py`, `app/reference/places_worth_going.csv`, 65,011 raw rows).

**Landmark tags kept:** town hall, courthouse, library, museum, viewpoint, attraction, visitor centre, memorial, monument, historic marker, plus a 2026-08-24 broadening for outdoor/natural destinations (hot spring, arch, cave entrance, waterfall, mine, ruins, fort, battlefield, wreck, lighthouse, alpine/wilderness hut, small nature reserve). Fire station and post office were cut before this landed — nobody drives to one.

**Trailheads and fire lookouts were removed 2026-08-25** ("trailheads shouldn't be marked as landmarks"; "lookouts in the mountains — not a landmark"). A trailhead is where you start going somewhere, not a destination itself; a fire lookout on a peak is already scored as that peak's summit. In the western-US OSM extract this pipeline reads from, `highway=trailhead` matched 4,166 objects and `man_made=tower` WHERE `tower:type=observation` matched 312 — both removed from the extraction's tag list (`LANDMARK_TAGS` in `scripts/build_places_seed.py`), dropping the landmark count from 30,408 to 25,930. This had to happen at extraction time, not load time: the seed CSV carries only `ref_type=landmark`, never the source OSM tag, so the loader has nothing to filter on — a load-time patch was not possible, which is why the seed needed a full rebuild.

**The seed is not pre-filtered to the US.** SOTA and POTA were both pulled over one bounding box (49.29N/25.8S/-125W/-93.5E) that, being a rectangle, also swept in northern Mexico and southern Canada. The loader (`app/places_seed.py`) excludes those at load time, not by re-pulling the CSV:

- **Summits:** by SOTA association code (confirmed against SOTA's own `/api/associations/` endpoint) — `XE2` (Mexico - North, 7,246 summits) and `VE5`/`VE6`/`VE7` (Saskatchewan/Alberta/British Columbia, 703 summits) excluded. `K0M` looks like an odd one out next to the W-prefixed codes but is genuinely USA - Minnesota, not a typo.
- **Parks:** by POTA's own reference prefix — `CA-` (328) and `MX-` (70) excluded; `US-` kept.
- **Landmarks:** not filtered — every row's coordinates fall inside the real US-Mexico and US-Canada borders already (the OSM extract they came from is western-US-only), verified rather than assumed.

Kept after the country filter, as of the 2026-08-25 rebuild: **8,082 summits, 44,037 parks (2,224 POTA-only, 3,467 POTA with a PAD-US boundary, 38,346 PAD-US-sourced), 25,930 landmarks.** The summit count is much lower than the pre-2026-08-24 pull because `SUMMIT_MIN_SOTA_POINTS` now filters to SOTA 8+; the park count is much higher because PAD-US became a park SOURCE, not just a boundary lookup.

**Park boundaries are not fully matched.** POTA publishes centre points only; a boundary is matched from PAD-US afterward (`scripts/build_places_seed.py`'s `match_parks()`), and PAD-US-sourced parks arrive with their own boundary already. Measured on the current seed CSV, **41,813 of 44,037 parks carry a boundary and 2,224 (5.1%) do not** — the unmatched remainder is exactly the POTA-only rows. (The 65.5%/34.5% split recorded here earlier was measured before PAD-US became a park source in its own right.) An unmatched park is not treated as small — it scores like a summit, its point's own square (now expanded to a 3x3 block, see Containment below), always active, never rotating. Of the 41,813 parks that do carry a boundary, 10,657 are at or above one grid cell in area and 31,156 are smaller than one cell (score a 3x3 point block, and rotate like a landmark — see below) — the PAD-US city/county parks that arrived in bulk are overwhelmingly in that second group.

**Elementary schools were dropped from the park source, 2026-09-07 (Matt's decision, narrowed same day).** School districts register plenty of their own campuses with PAD-US as "local parks" (`Des_Tp` `LP`/`LREC` — see the local/city/county park stage above), and the game should not send a player to stand outside an elementary school with an antenna. `scripts/build_places_seed.py`'s `_compile_exclude_park_name_re()` — the same name-pattern filter that already dropped community gardens and utility parcels — now also drops elementary/primary/grade-school/K-6/K-8/preschool/pre-K/intermediate-school names, checked **before** an explicit keep-list for junior high, middle school, senior high, college, university, institute, and seminary names (order matters: a real combined campus can be named e.g. "Lincoln Elementary and Middle School", matching both lists as substrings of one name, so the drop test has to run first or a later-grade word in the same name could rescue an elementary). A name this can't classify either way — mostly real parks that merely happen to be *named after* a school ("Blackwell School National Park", "Elgin School House State Park") — defaults to kept, since deleting a real park to catch an elementary school is a far worse mistake than the reverse.

The first cut of this rule (same day) also dropped junior high and middle schools; Matt reversed that part once the reachable-ring credit change below shipped, because a school now credits from the public sidewalk outside its fence rather than requiring anyone to set foot on campus — that removed the actual objection for every grade except the youngest, where a low fence around small children still isn't a place worth routing a game to. Confirmed: "West Junior High School" (Boise, PADUS-84489, 0.12 km², 5 points) survives the filter. Measured against the seed CSV at the time this landed: 801 elementary-and-below parks dropped, 386 kept via the explicit junior-high/middle/senior-high/college keep list, 245 kept unclassified (school-adjacent names the pattern couldn't resolve either way). Applied to `ref_type == 'park'` only — landmarks and summits are untouched. Park count after this change: 43,236 (was 44,037).

## Containment: which square scores

Credit zones are reachable-ring based, not pure geometry (changed 2026-09-07, "reward the trip, not the trespass") — the original point-only and >50%-boundary-only rules meant a place could be geometrically real but practically uncapturable: a landmark's point inside a fenced schoolyard, or a park whose boundary is mostly closed to the public (Rocky Mountain Arsenal National Wildlife Refuge, 60 km², is the motivating case — the >50%-inside-the-boundary rule excluded its entire public-accessible perimeter along with the genuinely closed interior). Matt's framing: *"they can't ENTER it but they still WENT to it."* So every place type except summits now credits a ring of cells one square wider than its own boundary test:

- **Landmark, or a park with no matched boundary, or a park smaller than one grid cell:** the square the place's own point falls in, **plus the 8 surrounding squares** (a 3x3 block). One ring, not two.
- **Park at or above one grid cell, boundary matched:** every square whose area intersects the boundary **at all** (no longer >50% inside it), **plus one ring of squares outward** from that set.
- **Summit: unchanged, own square only** (or its terrain-qualified set, see Weekly rotation below) — deliberately **not** ring-expanded. A SOTA activation requires physically reaching the summit; that is the point of the game mode, not an oversight to fix.

The ring is computed as a set expansion over cell indices (`app/places_seed.py`'s `_ring_expand()`), not a second geometry pass, so it stays cheap even for the largest boundary-matched parks — helped by the fact that a park's stored `geom` is already clipped to ~6km around its own point at seed-build time (see above), so a 10,000 km² national forest contributes the same few hundred cells to this test as any other candidate, not its true footprint. `place_cell`'s `PRIMARY KEY (place_id, cell_id)` plus an `INSERT OR IGNORE` load absorbs any overlap between a place's base set and its ring, so ring expansion can never produce a duplicate row. `app/place_scoring.py` is unchanged by any of this — it was already, and remains, a single indexed lookup on `place_cell`; the reach is entirely a seed-time decision.

## Weekly rotation (decided 2026-08-24, retuned 2026-08-25)

Summits and boundary-backed parks — anything that does not rotate above — are **always active**. Landmarks and small parks **rotate weekly**, flipping on the same Wednesday reset as everything else, so a town's handful of live places changes from week to week rather than every landmark within reach being live all the time.

**The draw is deterministic, not random-per-player.** It is seeded from the week's own identifier (`app/place_rotation.py`, `hashlib.sha256("places-rotation:<week_start>")` — not Python's `hash()`, which is process-randomized and would not agree between two servers or two restarts). Every player sees the same live set for a given week, and the same week computed twice — on any machine, at any time — produces the identical draw. Computed once and cached in `place_week` the first time anything needs it that week (a scoring ping or a map request), not recomputed on every read.

**Two constraints on the draw:**

- **1-mile minimum spacing** (`MIN_SPACING_MILES`). No two live rotating places within 1 mile of each other, checked globally (not just within one region cell — two candidates a short walk apart but on opposite sides of a cell boundary must not both get picked).
- **Per-region quota.** The play area is divided into region cells sized **18 miles** on a side (`ROTATION_CELL_MILES`), each allowed up to **15 live rotating places** (`ROTATION_QUOTA_PER_CELL = 15`). A cell with fewer candidates than the quota gets all of them; a cell with many (a city downtown) fills more of its quota, up to what the 1-mile spacing floor still allows once that many are competing for room.

**Retuned 2026-08-25 against a real complaint** ("I shouldn't have to travel more than 10 minutes between spaces… entire towns have 4 available with the next closest 45 minutes away", and separately "even in larger cities I can't find the places" / "very very few landmarks and local parks"). The 2026-08-24 tuning (quota 1→5) had been measured against a preview whose play area was misconfigured to roughly Idaho and northern Utah — about a tenth of the real board — and against "does Twin Falls get more than one place" rather than against how a player actually experiences density.

Re-measured on the real play area (49.29N/25.8S/-125W/-93.5E, 61,563 active rotating candidates: 30,408 landmarks + ~31,155 small parks) against the yardstick that matches the complaint: **from any populated place, do the live ROTATING places (landmarks + small parks only — summits and boundary-backed parks don't count here) within a 40-mile radius (Twin Falls to Burley, a real answer to "how far would I actually drive") add up to at least 100 points (one week's per-person cap)?** Measured against the 12,136 real town anchors inside the play area in `app/reference/places.csv` (the file also carries a 1-degree margin strip with no seed data in it — those margin rows read as false zero-supply and are excluded from this measurement).

| | quota 5 / spacing 3mi (old) | quota 15 / spacing 1mi (current) |
|---|---|---|
| live rotating places nationwide | 8,303–8,457 | 16,267 |
| towns reaching 100 rotating-tier points within 40mi | 84.5% (10,250/12,136) | 91.3% (11,080/12,136) |
| … + boundary-backed big parks as fallback for the rest | — | +7.6% (927 more towns) |
| still short even with the big-park fallback | — | 1.06% (129 towns) — real empty country: Big Bend, the Dakota/Montana high plains, the Nebraska Sandhills |

Sweeping quota alone (spacing held at 3mi) moved the pass rate only 84.8%→84.9% before flattening completely at quota 25 — quota was not the real constraint. Sweeping spacing alone (quota held at 5) moved it 84.8%→93.3% at 0.5mi — spacing was. Of the candidate-slots the old 3-mile rule discarded, roughly 79% were in cells with 10 or fewer candidates to begin with: it was thinning already-thin rural clusters, not "crowded cities" as intended. `ROTATION_CELL_MILES` (18mi) was not changed — cell size was never the measured problem.

**Repeats are a fallback, not a preference.** Last week's picks are sorted to the back of their cell's candidate list; a place only repeats if nothing else in its cell clears the spacing check against everything already chosen elsewhere.

### Coverage re-measured after the effort-based rescore (2026-08-25)

The 91.3%/98.9% figures above were measured under the old flat 5/25/100 model. Re-run against the same yardstick (40-mile radius, 100-point cap, the 12,136 in-play-area town anchors, quota 15 / spacing 1mi unchanged) after both Change 1 (effort-based scoring) and Change 2 (trailhead/lookout landmarks dropped, 30,408 → 25,930):

| | old model (91.3% baseline) | effort-scored (current) |
|---|---|---|
| towns reaching 100 rotating-tier points within 40mi | 91.3% (11,080/12,136) | **82.47% (10,008/12,136)** |
| … + boundary-backed big parks as fallback | +7.6% (927 more towns) → 98.9% | **+14.93% (1,812 more towns) → 97.40% (11,820/12,136)** |
| still short even with the big-park fallback | 1.06% (129 towns) | **2.60% (316 towns)** |

**This is a real regression, stated plainly, not papered over.** The rotating-tier-alone figure drops about 8.8 points (91.3% → 82.5%) — expected, and exactly what "city parks and landmarks dropping from 25/5 to a flat 5 will hurt town coverage" predicts: a small city park or town-hall landmark used to be able to single-handedly cover a chunk of the weekly cap for someone who never left town; now it takes 20 of them. The boundary-backed-park fallback (large, PAD-US-matched parks, mostly *not* inside any town's radius and so still worth their full 25) recovers most of the gap, landing at 97.40% — but that is still about 1.5 points under the old combined 98.9%, and 2.4x as many towns (316 vs 129) fall short of the cap even with every fallback counted. Both changes contributed: the effort model does the bulk of it (a town's own supply is worth far less now), and the landmark tag narrowing (Change 2, −4,478 landmarks nationwide) shrinks the rotating candidate pool on top of that.

No rotation setting (quota, spacing, cell size) was touched to compensate — the brief was to report this number, not tune around it.

**Preview without committing:** the admin panel's Places section (`app/admin_ops.py`'s `/api/admin/places/preview`, `frontend/admin.html`/`admin.js`) runs the same draw for any week — including the current one, or a hypothetical future one — without writing to `place_week`, so an operator can sanity-check density (candidates per cell, densest cells, a sample of the draw) before or long after a week actually happens.

## Why per person rather than per team

It matches the existing rule that the first new person to paint a square earns extra, because more people beats one person going back and forth.

## Why the weekly cap does the work

- It keeps check-ins relevant: a full week of places is four check-ins, not ten.
- It stops landmark density deciding anything, because a city grinder and a mountaineer reach the same ceiling.
- It keeps the summit attractive, since one trip beats twenty town halls.

## Seeding is the real balance knob, not the point values

A narrow OpenStreetMap tag list, not every point of interest.

**In (as actually pulled — see "The seed, as actually pulled" above):** town hall, courthouse, library, museum, viewpoint, attraction, visitor centre, historic marker, monument, memorial, plus the 2026-08-24 outdoor/natural broadening.

**Out:** fire station and post office (cut before the seed landed — nobody drives to one), trailheads and fire lookouts (cut 2026-08-25, see above), schools, hospitals, churches, playgrounds, anything on private land, anything you would not tell a stranger to drive to at night.

The test is permanent, publicly accessible, distinctive. Narrow is the recoverable direction — adding tags later gives people new places, pruning later takes credits from people who already earned them.

## Seed first, submissions maybe never

The lesson from Ingress and Pokémon Go is that Niantic seeded from existing databases first and opened player submission years later, with players reviewing each other. User submission makes the operator the referee: someone submits their own driveway, or a real place on private land, and the game starts telling people to trespass.

## What it changes about the honors

Explorer originally became most Explorer points that month, instead of most squares nobody had ever claimed. That did not last: the 100-point weekly cap meant everyone who played seriously finished a month within the same narrow band, so the points version separated nobody either — the same flaw that retired Most Consistent (see "Decisions reversed" in `docs/features/long-season.md`). The Explorer AWARD is now retired outright — the season-long Explorer ranking below is untouched by that and could not have been month-scoped anyway, since it feeds the season total score — replaced by three awards that count VISITS rather than points — Tourist (landmarks), Park Hopper (parks), Peak Tagger (summits) — one row in `place_activation` of the matching type inside the month is one visit, and the place's point value is never read. The weekly cap still does the work: it makes the three mutually exclusive in practice, since a single remote summit spends the whole week, so each one describes a different way to play rather than a different view of the same grind. Frontier is unaffected by any of this — it kept counting squares beyond city limits and dropped its virgin-ground restriction (which only existed so Frontier would be a strict subset of the old squares-based Explorer) before Explorer was ever redefined around points.

**Built so far:** a place credit (`place_activation`) adds to the team total (`app/mc_scoring.team_place_points`, folded into `team_totals()`) and to a personal running figure (`explorer_points` on each player row in `/api/v1/players`), both scoped to the season by `awarded_at` falling inside it — this running figure is unrelated to the monthly awards above and is untouched by any of their redefinitions. It is what "Explorer" now means: a season-long activity ranking (the Explorer tab under Top Operators) that contributes to total score, which is precisely why it cannot be reset or judged per month. `app/results.py`'s monthly awards are built: Tourist, Park Hopper, and Peak Tagger each count that type's visits inside the month, and Frontier keeps its own out-of-town square count.

## Considered and dropped

- An "infrastructure" award for distinct repeaters personally heard.
- Keeping Explorer points out of the team total entirely.
- Making the check-in a multiplier on the week's places — rejected because a bonus for showing up is a penalty for not, and the person out of signal on Wednesday is the one who played hardest.
- Scaling park credit by park size — rejected because SOTA and POTA themselves do not; a pocket park and a wilderness both count as one activation.
